The EU AI Act, read by a US financial firm

By Kunjar Bhaduri · Published October 27, 2026 · Last reviewed October 27, 2026

A US lender with no office in Europe can still be inside the EU AI Act. Article 2(1)(c) reaches providers and deployers established outside the EU "where the output produced by the AI system is used in the Union".

I designed the Autonomy Ladder, an AI-governance framework for regulated industries, with mappings to the EU AI Act. What follows is the order I'd read the Act in if I ran technology at a US bank, lender or insurer. It is a reading of the text, not legal advice, and your counsel should have the last word.

Step 1: where is the output used?

Article 2 has three doors that matter for a US firm. You're in if you place an AI system on the EU market, wherever you're based. You're in if you deploy one from an establishment in the EU, such as a branch or subsidiary. And you're in if you're outside the EU but the output of your system is used in the Union.

The third door is the easiest to miss. A model running in Virginia that scores applicants in Ireland is producing output used in the Union. Start with an inventory: every system that scores, prices or decides something about a person, and where those people are.

Step 2: is the system on Annex III?

Annex III lists the high-risk uses. Two of its lines land in finance:

  • point 5(b): systems "intended to be used to evaluate the creditworthiness of natural persons or establish their credit score, with the exception of AI systems used for the purpose of detecting financial fraud";
  • point 5(c): systems "intended to be used for risk assessment and pricing in relation to natural persons in the case of life and health insurance".

Read the fraud carve-out closely. A fraud model is outside 5(b). A model that does both fraud screening and credit decisions is a harder call, and that is a design question as much as a legal one. Splitting the two functions cleanly makes the answer easier to defend.

Step 3: which side are you on?

The provider develops the system, or has it developed, and puts it on the market or into service under its own name. The deployer uses it under its own authority. A bank that buys a credit model from a vendor is a deployer. A bank that builds its own and runs it is acting as both.

For high-risk systems, Articles 9 to 15 set what the system needs: a risk management system, data governance, technical documentation, record-keeping, information for deployers, human oversight, and Article 15's "Accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity". Article 26 sets the deployer's duties, and Article 27 adds one that names points 5(b) and 5(c): before deploying such a system, the deployer must assess its impact on fundamental rights. If you only deploy, your vendor contract should give you what you'll need to meet both.

Step 4: when does each part apply?

  • Prohibited practices: since 2 February 2025, except two bans the AI Omnibus added, on AI systems that generate or manipulate non-consensual intimate material or child sexual abuse material, which apply from 2 December 2026.
  • Transparency duties: since 2 August 2026.
  • Annex III high-risk rules: from 2 December 2027. The original date was 2 August 2026. The AI Omnibus, in force since 27 July 2026, moved it.

The decision tree

  1. Is the system's output used in the EU, or do you place it on the EU market or deploy it from an EU establishment? If no to all three, none of the three doors opens for it. Write down why.
  2. Does it evaluate creditworthiness or set a credit score for individuals, or assess risk and price life or health insurance for individuals? If no, check the other Annex III areas, the prohibited practices and the transparency duties, then stop.
  3. Is it used only to detect financial fraud? If yes, it falls outside point 5(b). Document how you know it does nothing else.
  4. Are you the provider, the deployer, or both? List the duties for each role you hold.
  5. Work back from 2 December 2027. Logs, data lineage and human-oversight design take the longest. Start there.

What I would do on Monday

  1. Inventory every system that scores, prices or decides about a person, and where those people are.
  2. Mark provider vs deployer (or both) and whether Annex III 5(b) or 5(c) applies, including the fraud carve-out.
  3. Use the sixteen months to 2 December 2027 to build logs, data lineage and human oversight, not to wait.
