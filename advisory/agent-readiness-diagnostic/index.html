Who it's for

CIOs, CTOs, COOs and heads of digital at community banks, credit unions, insurers and payments companies who have one of these on the table:

  • an AI idea or a vendor's "agent" pitch, and no agreed way to judge it;
  • a risk, compliance or audit team that will want evidence before anything goes live;
  • a board or examiner asking what the AI plan is.

What you get

  1. A readiness score for the chosen workflow across six areas: business value, data access and quality, systems and integration, controls and model risk, vendor and third-party risk, and people and process.
  2. An autonomy map. Each step of the workflow is placed on a level, from "the system suggests and a person decides" to "the system acts and a person reviews after". It uses the Autonomy Ladder, the framework I designed and open-sourced.
  3. A control register in a spreadsheet: each risk, the control for it, the evidence you would keep, and who owns it, written against the guidance your reviewers already work from.
  4. A build, buy or wait view, including where the vendors you are already talking to fit.
  5. A 90-day pilot plan with scope, success measures, team and a fixed-price range.
  6. A readout with your executive sponsor, plus the written report.

What changes by sector

Banks and credit unions

The questions are usually about model risk and the vendor behind the tool. If the workflow touches a credit decision, the map shows where the adverse-action notice comes from under ECOA and Regulation B, and which step a person must own.

Insurers

Claims and underwriting are where an agent's authority matters most. The map marks which decisions stay with an adjuster or underwriter, and the register lines up with the AI expectations your state regulator has set, including the NAIC model bulletin on insurers' use of AI systems where your state has adopted it.

Payments firms

Disputes, fraud holds and sanctions screening are the steps where an automated mistake costs money or breaks a rule fast. The register shows who can override the system, how quickly, and what record is kept when they do.

Questions

Is this an audit or a compliance review?

No. It is a decision document for your leadership team, with an evidence trail your own risk, audit and legal reviewers can test. It is not a legal opinion or a compliance sign-off.

Do you need access to our systems?

No. The work runs on documents, interviews and screen shares. Masked or test records are fine, and I never ask for production credentials.

What if the answer is not yet?

Then the report says why and what would need to change first, so the next budget conversation starts from evidence rather than a vendor demo.

Can our vendor's product be part of the review?

Yes. Bring the vendor's materials and I will place their product on the same autonomy map and control register as everything else.
