1. Private equity sponsors

Tech Health snapshot for portfolio companies

A fixed-scope review of how healthy a company's technology is, written for the sponsor and the CEO rather than for the IT team. It is built for companies that run on software but do not sell it, where nobody on the board can easily tell whether the systems, vendors and backups would hold up.

Who it is for
Operating partners, deal teams and portfolio CEOs. Use it before close as part of diligence, in the first months after close, or before an exit.
What you get
  • A read-only review of the main systems, cloud accounts, user access, backups and recovery, key vendors and contracts, and any code the company owns. I do not change anything in the company's environment.
  • Short interviews with the CEO, the CFO, whoever runs IT, and the main outside IT provider.
  • A Tech Health Index score from 0 to 100, built from the same areas for every company, so a sponsor can compare portfolio companies on one scale.
  • A written readout that ranks the risks by their effect on cash, customers and the investment plan, with a named owner and a rough cost and timing to fix each one.
  • A one-hour readout meeting with the sponsor and management.
Timeline
About three weeks from read-only access to the readout.
After the snapshot
  • Portfolio monitoring. A refreshed score for each company every quarter and a quarterly review with each company's leadership, so the sponsor sees whether the risks are actually being closed.
  • CTO on call. A senior technology leader the sponsor and portfolio CEOs can call about vendor renewals, incidents, key hires, and diligence on add-on acquisitions.
Fees
The snapshot is a fixed fee, quoted after a 15-minute call. Monitoring is billed per company per month. CTO on call is a monthly retainer.
How to start
Book 15 minutes and tell me the company's size, what it does, and whether the deal is before or after close. You can also use the form on the home page and choose PE sponsor / Tech Health snapshot.

2. Leadership teams and boards

Fractional or interim CTO, CIO or Chief AI Officer

I take the technology leadership seat for a defined period, or for part of each week, and own the decisions that come with it. This is the same operating work described in the track record on the home page: turnarounds, platform rebuilds and recovery after an incident.

Who it is for
PE-backed and financial services companies that are between technology leaders, in a turnaround, rebuilding a platform, or not yet ready for a full-time hire.
What you get
  • A 100-day plan. In the first two weeks I follow one process the business cannot afford to lose, such as billing or service delivery, and find who owns it, which vendor can interrupt it and when it was last restored.
  • By day 30, a cost and headcount baseline that finance and operations agree on, decision rights, and an incident escalation path.
  • By day 60, one bottleneck fixed, a tested restore, and a short list of the risks that remain.
  • By day 100, a funded roadmap, a named owner for each decision still open, and a handover to whoever runs technology next, including help hiring a permanent leader if that is the plan.
Timeline
Interim roles are full time for an agreed period. Fractional roles are a set number of days each week, billed monthly. Both start with the 100-day plan, and the length is agreed before starting.
How to start
Book 15 minutes. I am also open to permanent CTO, CIO and Chief AI Officer roles.

4. Boards, risk leaders and AI teams

AI governance advisory and advisory boards

I wrote Autonomy Ladder, an open-source framework for deciding how much authority an AI system gets. It runs from A0, where the system only reads and recommends, to A4, where it runs in production with monitoring and escalation. I use it to help a company write down what each AI system may do, how that limit is enforced, and who can take the authority away.

Who it is for
Boards, risk and audit committees, and technology and risk leaders at banks, insurers, wealth and lending firms. Also AI companies that sell into those firms, and startups that want an advisory board member with regulated-industry operating experience.
What you get
  • A map of each AI action in a workflow to a rung on the ladder. One application can need different rungs for retrieving documents, recording a verified field and making a credit decision.
  • Control tests run against the real system, such as an expired approval, a blocked write and a missing audit event, with the results checked against the system of record.
  • A plain-language briefing for the board or committee on what the systems can do today and what evidence would justify more authority.
  • For advisory boards, a standing seat with regular meetings and time between them for questions.
Limits
The ladder is a design discipline for governing agent authority. It does not establish regulatory compliance or replace model validation.
Timeline
A governance review covers one system or workflow and takes about two weeks. Advisory board roles follow the company's meeting schedule.
How to start
Run the free A0 to A4 self-score, then book 15 minutes to go through the result.
